Life of Prophet Muhammad (peace and blessings be upon him)

Lineage, Ancestry & Nobility
- Direct descendant of Prophet Ibrahim (Abraham) and Prophet Ismail (Ishmael) through the noble Adnani Arab branch.
- Belongs to the foremost tribe of Arabia, Quraysh, within the honored clan of Banu Hashim.
- Father: Abdullah ibn Abd al-Muttalib (died in Yathrib before Muhammad's birth).
- Mother: Aminah bint Wahb of the Banu Zuhrah clan.
- Paternal Grandfather: Abdul-Muttalib ibn Hashim, the custodian of the Kaaba and Zamzam.

Birth and Early Childhood (570 - 582 CE)
- Born on Monday, 12th of Rabi' al-Awwal in the Year of the Elephant (Am al-Fil, approximately 570 CE) in Mecca.
- Nursed by Thuwaybah, then entrusted to Halimah al-Sa'diyyah of the Banu Sa'd tribe in the desert air for four years.
- Incident of the Opening of the Breast (Shaqq al-Sadr) occurred during his stay with Halimah.
- Returned to his mother Aminah at age four; she passed away at Al-Abwa on a return journey from Yathrib when he was six years old.
- Taken into loving guardianship by his grandfather Abdul-Muttalib until he died when Muhammad was eight years old.
- Passed into the care of his benevolent uncle Abu Talib, who protected him with dedication for over forty years.
- Journeyed to Syria with Abu Talib's trade caravan at age twelve, where the Christian monk Bahira recognized the marks of prophethood between his shoulders.

Youth, Character & Marriage (582 - 610 CE)
- Known throughout Mecca for his flawless integrity, honesty, modesty, and justice, earning the titles Al-Sadiq (The Truthful) and Al-Amin (The Trustworthy).
- Participated in the Alliance of the Virtuous (Hilf al-Fudul), a solemn covenant formed to protect the oppressed and guarantee rights of strangers in Mecca.
- Managed the trade caravan of the noble, wealthy businesswoman Khadijah bint Khuwaylid to Syria with outstanding profit and ethical distinction.
- Impressed by his noble character, Khadijah proposed marriage; they married when he was 25 and she was 40.
- Khadijah bore him six children: Al-Qasim, Abdullah, Zaynab, Ruqayyah, Umm Kulthum, and Fatimah. (His son Ibrahim was born later in Medina to Maria al-Qibtiyyah).
- Arbitrated the placement of the Black Stone (Al-Hajar al-Aswad) during the rebuilding of the Kaaba at age 35, averting inter-tribal warfare through brilliant consensus by placing it on a cloth lifted by all tribal leaders together.

The First Revelation & Dawn of Prophethood (610 CE)
- Developed a love for solitude and contemplation, retreating to the Cave of Hira atop Jabal al-Nur during Ramadan.
- In Ramadan 610 CE (at age 40), the Archangel Jibril (Gabriel) appeared to him with the first verses of revelation:
  "Read in the name of your Lord who created—Created man from a clinging substance. Read, and your Lord is the Most Generous—Who taught by the pen—Taught man that which he knew not." (Surah Al-Alaq 96:1-5).
- Trembling with awe, he returned to Khadijah, who comforted him with historical words: "Never! By Allah, Allah will never humiliate you. You maintain ties of kinship, support the weak, earn for the destitute, honor your guests, and assist in all good causes."
- Khadijah's cousin, the blind scholarly scholar Waraqah ibn Nawfal, confirmed that this was the same Namus (angel) sent to Musa (Moses).

The Makkan Period: Secret and Open Dawah (610 - 622 CE)
- Secret Propagation (3 years): Earliest converts included Khadijah (first believer and woman), Ali ibn Abi Talib (first youth), Zayd ibn Harithah (first freed slave), and Abu Bakr al-Siddiq (first free adult male).
- Open Preaching: Allah commanded: "And warn your closest kindred" (26:214). The Prophet ascended Mount Al-Safa and summoned Quraysh clans, warning them of the divine reckoning.
- Persecution of Muslims: Quraysh unleashed boycott, torture, and brutality against vulnerable converts (Sumayyah bint Khayyat became the first martyr, Bilal ibn Rabah tortured in desert heat, Khabbab, Ammar).
- First Migration to Abyssinia (615 CE): Prophet advised a group of Muslims to seek refuge under the just Christian King Ashamah (Al-Najashi) in Abyssinia, where Ja'far ibn Abi Talib recited Surah Maryam to the Negus.
- Conversions of Hamzah ibn Abd al-Muttalib and Umar ibn al-Khattab (616 CE) brought great strength and public prayer to the Kaaba.
- The Social and Economic Boycott (617 - 619 CE): Quraysh placed Banu Hashim and Banu al-Muttalib under harsh siege in the canyon of Shi'b Abi Talib for three years until the parchment was consumed by termites.
- The Year of Sorrow (Am al-Huzn, 619 CE): The Prophet's beloved wife Khadijah and guardian uncle Abu Talib died within months of each other, stripping him of internal emotional sanctuary and external political protection.
- The Mission to Ta'if (619 CE): Traveled on foot to invite the Thaqif tribe; their leaders mocked him and commanded town youths to stone him until his sandals ran with blood. When Angel Jibril offered to crush them between the two mountains, the Prophet replied with mercy: "No, I hope that Allah will bring forth from their loins those who worship Allah alone."
- The Night Journey and Heavenly Ascension (Al-Isra wal-Mi'raj, 620/621 CE): Miraculously transported from Mecca's Sacred Mosque to Al-Aqsa Mosque in Jerusalem on Al-Buraq, leading all prophets in prayer, then ascended through the seven heavens to Sidrat al-Muntaha, where the five daily prayers were established.
- Pledges of Al-Aqabah (621 & 622 CE): Pilgrims from Yathrib (Medina) embraced Islam and pledged allegiance (Bay'ah) to protect the Prophet, sending Mus'ab ibn Umayr as the first ambassador of Islam.

The Great Migration (Hijrah) to Medina (622 CE)
- Quraysh plotted an assassination using one warrior from every clan to disperse blood guilt among all tribes.
- Commanded by Allah, Prophet Muhammad instructed Ali to sleep in his bed to return trusts to their owners, and slipped away unnoticed with Abu Bakr.
- Sheltered in the Cave of Thawr for three days, where Abu Bakr expressed fear and the Prophet consoled him: "Do not grieve; indeed Allah is with us" (9:40).
- Arrived at Quba on 12th Rabi' al-Awwal 622 CE, founding Masjid Quba (the first mosque in Islam).
- Entered Yathrib (renamed Madinat al-Nabi, the City of the Prophet).
- Established the Prophet's Mosque (Al-Masjid al-Nabawi) and built ties of brotherhood (Mu'akhah) pairing each Muhajir (Meccan immigrant) with an Ansari (Medinan helper).
- Drafted the Constitution of Medina (Sahifat al-Madinah), the world's first written democratic constitution establishing mutual defense, religious freedom, and civil rights for Muslims, Jews, and pagan tribes.

The Medinan Period: Defense and Statehood (622 - 630 CE)
- Battle of Badr (17th Ramadan, 2 AH / 624 CE): 313 poorly equipped Muslims miraculously defeated 1,000 elite Quraysh warriors; major tyrants like Abu Jahl fell.
- Battle of Uhud (3 AH / 625 CE): Strategic battle where Muslims had early success, but archers abandoned the hill against orders, resulting in flanking attack by Khalid ibn al-Walid and the martyrdom of Hamzah.
- Battle of the Trench / Confederates (Al-Ahzab, 5 AH / 627 CE): A grand coalition of 10,000 enemy troops besieged Medina. Salman al-Farsi advised digging a deep defensive trench north of the city; siege disintegrated through divine wind and cold.
- Treaty of Hudaybiyyah (6 AH / 628 CE): A ten-year peace pact viewed by companions as harsh, yet hailed in the Quran as a "clear victory" (Fath Mubeen), allowing Islam to spread peaceful engagement throughout Arabia.
- Letters to World Leaders (7 AH / 628 CE): Dispatched official diplomatic envoys with royal letters bearing his silver seal to Heraclius of Byzantine, Chosroes of Persia, the Negus of Abyssinia, and the ruler of Alexandria.
- Battle of Khaybar (7 AH / 628 CE): Fortresses of Khaybar pacified and safe cultivation granted.

Conquest of Mecca & Tribal Unification (630 CE)
- In 8 AH (630 CE), after Quraysh allies violated the Treaty of Hudaybiyyah by attacking the allied Banu Khuza'ah, the Prophet marched on Mecca with 10,000 men.
- Mecca was conquered peacefully with virtually no bloodshed.
- Entered the Kaaba, shattered 360 surrounding idols while reciting: "Truth has come, and falsehood has departed. Indeed is falsehood, [by nature], ever bound to depart" (17:81).
- Stood before the terrified Quraysh who had persecuted him for twenty years and asked: "What do you think I will do with you?" They answered: "Noble brother and son of a noble brother." The Prophet declared unconditional amnesty: "Go, for you are free (Idh-habu fa antum al-tulaqa)."
- Battle of Hunayn and Siege of Ta'if: Consolidated southern Arabia; established peace and security across the peninsula.

The Farewell Pilgrimage & Passing (632 CE)
- Farewell Pilgrimage (Hajjat al-Wada, 10 AH / 632 CE): Over 124,000 companions accompanied the Prophet to Mecca.
- Delivered the universal Farewell Sermon (Khutbat al-Wada) on Mount Arafat, laying down foundational human rights:
  - Sanctity of life, property, and honor.
  - Abolition of blood feuds, usury (Riba), and racial supremacy: "An Arab has no superiority over a non-Arab, nor a non-Arab over an Arab; nor does a white person have superiority over a black person, nor a black person over a white person, except by piety and good action."
  - Treatment of women with gentleness and dignity as equal partners.
- Revelation of completion: "This day I have perfected for you your religion and completed My favor upon you and have approved for you Islam as your religion" (Surah Al-Ma'idah 5:3).
- Fell ill with severe fever in Safar 11 AH in Medina; appointed Abu Bakr to lead prayers in his stead.
- Passed away peacefully in the lap of his beloved wife Aisha on Monday, 12th Rabi' al-Awwal 11 AH (June 8, 632 CE) at the age of 63.
- His final whispered words were: "Bal al-Rafiq al-A'la" (Rather, the Highest Companion in Heaven).
- Buried in his room where he died, enclosed today within the Prophet's Mosque in Medina.
